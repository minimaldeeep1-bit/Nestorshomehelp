Kegerator Line Cleaning Schedule: How Often to Clean Beer Lines

By Vitalii Nestorenko · November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Clean your kegerator beer lines with a proper line cleaner about every two weeks, and at the very least every time you change a keg. Take the faucet apart and wash it on the same schedule, and add an acid clean about every three months to deal with beerstone. That is the routine the beer industry recommends, and it is the single best thing you can do to keep home draft beer tasting the way the brewer intended.

I brew beer professionally and install home bars in Calgary. With kegerators being a popular gift this time of year, here is how I would look after one, in plain steps.

Why dirty beer lines ruin good beer

Beer is food. Inside a line, yeast, bacteria and mineral deposits slowly build up on the walls. Two things follow: the beer picks up off flavours (sour, buttery, musty), and the buildup makes the beer foam, because rough or fouled surfaces release the CO2 out of the beer. Many people blame the regulator when the real culprit is the line or the faucet.

If you are chasing foam, check the cleaning first, then the pressure and temperature. I explain those in my kegerator CO2 pressure guide.

A realistic cleaning schedule

The Brewers Association's Draught Beer Quality Manual is the reference most bars follow. It recommends a full line cleaning every two weeks, and retailers often quote it as a minimum of every keg change. For a home setup, this is a sensible plan:

  • Every keg change (or every two weeks): run a caustic (alkaline) beer line cleaner through the lines, and take apart and wash the faucet and coupler.
  • Every three months: run an acid-based cleaner to remove beerstone, which caustic cleaner does not dissolve.
  • Every year or two: replace the beer line. Plastic and vinyl lines wear and hold on to flavours, and replacing them is cheap compared with a ruined keg.
  • A couple of times a year: fully disassemble the coupler, not just rinse it.

If you only pour a keg every month or two, do it at each keg change, because beer sitting in a line for weeks is exactly when the buildup happens.

What you need

  • A beer line cleaning kit: a hand pump or pressurized bottle with a cleaning keg adapter. A circulating pump is better still, since moving the solution cleans more thoroughly than just soaking.
  • Caustic (alkaline) beer line cleaner, and acid-based cleaner for the quarterly clean. Buy products made for draft lines, not household bleach or dish soap.
  • A faucet wrench (it often comes with the kegerator), a small brush, and a bucket.
  • Gloves and eye protection. These cleaners are caustic, so treat them with respect.

Step by step: cleaning the lines

  1. Finish or remove the keg and shut off the CO2 to the line you are cleaning.
  2. Remove the faucet with the wrench and set it aside for separate cleaning.
  3. Flush the line with cool water to push out the leftover beer.
  4. Fill the line with cleaning solution mixed to the strength on the product label. Many guides call for a soak of about 15 minutes, with the solution moved through the line if you have a pump.
  5. Rinse thoroughly with cool fresh water until no cleaner and no debris come out. Keep rinsing past the point where you think it is enough. Cleaner residue ruins the next keg.
  6. Reconnect everything, tap the new keg and pour off a little beer before you serve.

Never mix caustic and acid cleaners, and never run one straight after the other without a full water rinse in between.

Faucet and coupler: the part most people skip

Industry guides agree that the faucet is the most frequent source of problems, because it touches the air and sticky beer dries inside it. Unscrew it, take it fully apart, and soak the pieces in warm cleaning solution for 15 to 20 minutes. Scrub with a brush, clear the small vent holes, rinse well and put it back together. Do the coupler on the same visit. This takes ten minutes and often fixes a "mystery" foam problem on its own.

Keep a simple cleaning log

The Brewers Association offers a printable cleaning log with its manual, and the idea works at home too. Write the date on a piece of tape on the side of the kegerator each time you clean. It takes five seconds, and it stops you from guessing whether the lines were done two weeks ago or two months ago. Note the keg you tapped as well, so that if one keg tastes off you know whether the lines or the beer is to blame.

Signs your lines need cleaning now

  • Beer tastes sour, stale or buttery, especially the first pour of the day.
  • Foam appears even though the temperature and pressure are right.
  • You can see a film or slime at the faucet or in clear line.
  • The faucet feels sticky or slow.

FAQ

How often should I clean my kegerator lines?

Every two weeks or at each keg change, with an acid clean about every three months. If the kegerator sits unused for a while, clean it before you tap a new keg.

Can I just flush the lines with water?

Water removes beer but not the film. A proper line cleaner is needed to break down yeast, bacteria and deposits, and water alone leaves the buildup in place.

How long do beer lines last?

Many suppliers suggest replacing lines every one to two years, or sooner if they have been contaminated or you cannot get the flavour out.
