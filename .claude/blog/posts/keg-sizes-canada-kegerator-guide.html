Most kegs sold in Canada are metric: 20 L, 30 L and 50 L, with the 50 L being the most common commercial size. American-style sixtel and half-barrel kegs also turn up here. Before you buy a kegerator or a keg, check the keg's height, diameter and coupler against your fridge. That one check prevents most "it doesn't fit" surprises.

The common keg sizes

Commercial kegs are mainly made in 20 L, 30 L and 50 L, and the 50 L is the most common. In the United States the sizes are named as fractions of a barrel instead. A sixtel is about 19.5 L (sources round it slightly differently), and a half barrel is 15.5 US gallons, or 58.67 L. Canadian breweries often use the metric sizes, but the American ones are not rare, especially from craft brewers.

Here is a rough pour guide. The arithmetic is simple: litres divided by the size of your glass.

  • 20 L keg: about 42 pours of 473 mL (a standard "tall can" pint), or about 56 pours of 355 mL.
  • Sixtel (about 19.5 L): about the same as a 20 L, roughly 41 US pints.
  • 30 L keg: about 63 pours of 473 mL.
  • 50 L keg: about 105 pours of 473 mL.
  • Half barrel (58.67 L): about 124 US pints of 16 oz.

Expect a little less in real life. The first pour clears air from the line, and the last litre near the bottom is often foamy or cloudy.

Why most home kegerators take a smaller keg

A full-size kegerator or a converted fridge is built around a particular keg. A compact under-counter unit usually takes a sixtel or a 20 L. A 50 L or half barrel is much wider and heavier, so it needs a dedicated full-size unit or a custom build. Full kegs are heavy to move, and that matters on basement stairs.

My own advice is to buy the keg size your household can finish in a few weeks. Unpasteurized draft beer typically stays fresh for about 45 to 60 days when it is kept cold and dispensed with CO2 (Micro Matic and KegWorks both give this window), counted from the date it was kegged, not the date you bought it. Some vendors are more conservative, quoting four to eight weeks after tapping. If you drink a 20 L in two weeks, you will taste the beer as the brewer intended. A 50 L that takes three months is likely to lose its edge.

Couplers: the part that has to match

A keg's size and its coupler are two separate questions. In North America the standard is the D-system coupler, often just called "Sankey". It is used by most craft brewers and Canadian beers, and it fits sixtels, quarter barrels, half barrels, and 20, 30 and 50 L kegs. Imported kegs are the exception: they commonly use other fittings (A, G or S types), so a standard D coupler will not fit.

If you plan to buy a keg of imported beer, find out which coupler it needs before you buy. Some fridges come with a D coupler only, and a second coupler is a small extra cost.

Checking whether a keg fits your fridge

  1. Measure the interior of the fridge: height with the coupler attached, width and depth.
  2. Get the keg's real dimensions from the seller. Do not trust a general size chart. Retailers list different exterior sizes for the same nominal volume, and brewery requirements for returning kegs are different again.
  3. Allow height for the coupler. The coupler and gas and beer lines add height on top of the keg. Low-profile elbow fittings exist for tight fridges.
  4. Check the CO2 tank space. It sits inside or outside the fridge, and either way it needs a safe place where it cannot fall over.
  5. Think about the door seal. If the keg sticks out, the fridge will not close properly and the beer will warm up.

Calgary notes: basements, stairs and heating

A lot of Calgary home bars are in basements, which is a good cool place for beer. Two practical points. First, a full 50 L keg weighs well over 50 kg, so plan the route and do not move one alone on stairs. Second, keep the unit away from a furnace or a sunny window. A fridge works harder in a warm room, and a keg sitting in warm air changes how much foam you get when pouring.

Also leave space around the fridge for airflow. The kegerator installation guide covers ventilation, and the CO2 pressure guide covers what to set once the keg is connected.

Where to get kegs in the Calgary area

Many breweries and some liquor retailers can fill or sell kegs, but policies, deposits and sizes change. I won't list prices or shops here, because they are out of date quickly. Call the brewery or store and ask three things: what keg size and coupler they use, what the deposit is, and how long the beer has been in the keg.

FAQ

What keg size should I buy for a home kegerator?

A sixtel or 20 L is the practical choice for most homes. It fits compact units and gives roughly 40 pours, which most households can finish well inside the freshness window.

Are Canadian kegs different from American kegs?

Often, yes, in size. Canadian kegs are commonly 20, 30 or 50 L, while American ones are sixtels, quarter barrels and half barrels. Most use the same D-type coupler, but always confirm.

How long does beer last once the keg is tapped?

With CO2 and proper cold storage, unpasteurized beer commonly lasts several weeks, with sources giving anything from about four to eight weeks. Pumping air into the keg shortens that to hours, so use CO2.
